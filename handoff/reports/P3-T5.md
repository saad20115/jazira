### O-14 — the card could stay in `loading` for ever (`7663933`)
- **The likely cause:** three's `isReady()` asks the GPU process whether a program has linked. On a
  context lost before the first frame, the answer never turns true. The link waits and the link budget
  had no bound, and nothing listened for a lost context before the first frame. So the start polled
  for ever, and the card's own fallback came only after 45 s. I could not reproduce the verifier's two
  runs exactly, so this is the cause I found in the code, not one I saw in their trace.
- **What changed:**
  - **The card:** 8 s after the click, a start with no first frame falls back to the stills tier, with
    reason `timeout` (`SystemsCard.tsx`, `START_TIMEOUT_MS`). So within 10 s of the click the card is
    `live` or `stills`, with `data-viewer-fallback` set.
    - The stills come first. Disposing the aborted viewer, a long task on a loaded device, runs in a
      task of its own afterwards.
    - A first frame, or the start's own end, that comes after the fallback changes nothing.
    - Why 8 s, not 9 s: with 9 s, one full GPU e2e run on the final build failed the throttling test.
      The card reached stills later than 10 s after the click, because at 20× the timer and the synchronous
      disposal ran late. (That run's log was overwritten by the rerun; this sentence is its record.)
  - **Link waits:** `waitForLinks` and the link budget's `room` stop after `LINK_MAX_WAIT_MS`. A program
    still linking is then waited for by its first use, which the driver bounds (`precompile.ts`).
  - **A context lost while starting:** it aborts the start (every wait checks the signal), and the start
    rejects with the new reason `context-lost` at once, without waiting for the step in progress to
    notice (`createViewer.ts`, `failure.ts`, `ea52491`). In one repeat on `7663933`, a parse in
    progress let the card's deadline come first, and the reason was `timeout`.
  - **The other start-up waits were already bounded and are unchanged:** frames on time (1 s), fences
    (2 s each, the probe's included), idle slices (1 s), and the parse and transcode watchdog. The
    download is covered by the card's deadline.
- **The cost:** a start slower than the deadline now shows the stills tier instead of going live late.
  On my loaded machine, some clicks right after the jump went live later than the 8 s bound. In round 5,
  on a quieter machine, they went live well within it (see Known gaps).

### What made the long tasks (my traces of the round-4 build, the CTO gate flows with video on)
- **Not my JavaScript, mostly.** The long tasks after a click used little CPU for their length. The
  page's main thread sat inside plain WebGL calls (`shaderSource`, `getExtension`, `getContext`,
  draws), or in the page's own paint and layout, while it was not running.
- **What took its cores:** the GPU process compiles each new shader program (two D3D compiles) on
  worker threads. A physically based pixel shader takes a long time on the HD 530 box, and PMREM's GGX
  filter far longer. Round 4 created every program it could at once, so with the screencast running
  the page's main thread was left without a core. A click during preparation came right into that.
- **Smaller causes, in one task each:**
  - `getContext` plus three's start-up: each extension is a synchronous round trip, and right after the
    jump down the page the GPU process is still rasterising the new view;
  - the first draw uploading every vertex and index buffer of the model at once;
  - the label occlusion rays at the end of a flight (every hotspot against every mesh, all hits
    sorted).
- **One outside my files:** the page's own reveal and count-up code (`28sh15…js`, not P3-T5) forces a
  layout from a scheduler task. When the card has just added text, that layout includes shaping it.
  With the fixes below it no longer lines up with a starved main thread, and I did not see it make a
  long task in the final runs. It is named here in case the verifier does.

### O-12 — what changed (`1c09dc9`, `src/components/systems3d/viewer/`)
- **A link budget** (`precompile.ts` `createLinkBudget`): new programs are created only while fewer
  than a limit are still linking.
  - The limits are named constants (`LINKS_PREPARING`, `LINKS_LOADING`, `LINKS_SYSTEMS`): more while
    the hero is prepared unseen, fewer once the visitor waits, fewest for the systems.
  - Hero, studio (cube render) and system compiles all go through it, one object at a time.
  - A program three reuses counts once at most; a released one counts as done.
- **The renderer starts in steps** (`createViewer.ts`):
  - the import ends in its own task;
  - the context is created only once frames come on time again (at most a short wait);
  - the GPU name is read, and the model download starts at once (it no longer waits for three);
  - each extension that three and the KTX2 loader ask for is enabled in its own task;
  - then three's renderer, the loaders and PMREM, the parse, the material set-up, the camera and the
    canvas, each in its own task.
- **Buffers before the first frame** (`uploadGeometryInChunks`):
  - it draws the model a few MB at a time into a 1×1 viewport, everything else hidden;
  - a GPU fence comes between parts;
  - it runs after the hero's programs are linked and first used, so no draw waits for a link.
- **Order:**
  - the textures go up while the hero's programs are created;
  - the systems' programs start only after the timed probe frames, so nothing compiles beside them
    and the tier reflects the hero frame alone (round 4 compiled them during the probe);
  - the system the card entered goes first (new `enteredSystem` option);
  - once shown, the systems' warm-up waits during the swap and while the visitor waits for a flight;
  - it never runs while a system's look is on screen (as before).
- **Probe:** the second, confirming probe at pixel ratio 1 is skipped only when it would time the same
  frame and the first frames already meet the stills rate. So it can only avoid a stills fallback,
  never cause one. Otherwise as before.
- **Labels** (`systemStage.ts`):
  - an occlusion ray stops at the first hit;
  - the rays of one round are spread over frames within a small time budget (at least one per frame);
  - a label shows once its own ray finds it clear (before, one frame decided all of them).
- **Cost:** a click during preparation now waits longer under the push-in. The hero links a few
  programs at a time, and the GPU process keeps cores free for the page. See Known gaps.

### Q6 — the preparation trigger (`5d739f5`, `SystemsCard.tsx`)
- From the page's `load` event and the idle period after it, the card watches with a two-viewport

  Lighthouse. With the scroll condition, a page left at its top fetches nothing. See Question 9.
